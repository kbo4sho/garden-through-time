Scope: the original 3D oakleaf hydrangea's connected woody shoots and petioles, supple leaf surfaces, and credible irregular canopy in the integrated editorial bed. ## EVIDENCE

I read only the three clean briefs in `tmp/gauntlet-brief/` and inspected the running artifact at `http://127.0.0.1:4179/` using headless Google Chrome through Playwright. I did not inspect implementation, git history, builder reasoning, or previous reviews. Captures are my own fresh browser screenshots, kept under `branch-checkpoint/`. No scene settings or lighting were changed.

I captured and visually inspected the 3-, 5-, and 7-plant templates at day 15 (January 15), 135 (May 15), 200 (July 19), and 290 (October 17), at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844. The four-view desktop presents the selected hydrangea in the lower-right seasonal detail without requiring the editor. Raw filenames encode viewport, plant count, and day. `detail-{day}.png` captures the actual, ordinary-size selected detail canvas. `peek-{day}.png` and `restored-{day}.png` record a limited portrait drag and release for the three-plant template.

The connected structure is legible. In `desktop-3-15.png`, the main canes branch into finer lateral shoots instead of ending in a floating foliage cloud. Their asymmetric spread, varying heights, and returning dried heads establish a persistent recognizable shrub across the tested dates. Ground contact and the soft cast shadow are coherent with the other plants. In the leafed states, small connecting stalks are visible especially on the low front limbs. I did not see an obvious detached leaf cloud or repeated horizontal shelves.

The remaining failing appearance is in the leaf surfaces. At normal portrait size in `desktop-3-135.png` and `desktop-3-200.png`, the conspicuous low front hydrangea leaves (approximately x=365–470, y=625–695) read as broad, opaque, nearly flat lobed shapes. Their visible surface modulation is weak relative to their hard, angular outlines. Several upper leaves (around x=505–590, y=365–425 in May) turn into very dark, blunt angular silhouettes. The composition has actual depth, but these individual blades still contribute a rigid paper-leaf appearance rather than the supple, unevenly cupped and twisted surfaces required by the fidelity brief. This does not require zooming into a source asset to notice.

`peek-135.png` changes overlap and reveals more depth in the branch arrangement, but the low outer leaves continue to read as broad plates as the view turns. `restored-135.png` returns to the authored framing. The selected detail in `detail-135.png` and `detail-200.png` remains recognizable and connected but reduces the same stiff lobed silhouettes; its small presentation size does not independently demonstrate fine leaf curvature or convincing transmitted light.

The denser 5- and 7-plant beds retain coherent placement and lighting. The hydrangea's lower foliage is increasingly obscured by foreground plants, so they cannot overturn the clearer three-plant observation. The phone matrix keeps a recognizable whole garden, names, sender, readable date, and timeline on screen at all twelve combinations. Its reduced scale conceals much of the individual blade appearance and therefore does not close the desktop gap.

The seasonal matrix shows leaflessness and persistent woody structure in January, foliage in May, flowers in July, and warmer weathered coloration in October, without making the other plants change into the same state. This checkpoint does not certify the exact horticultural windows; no source-to-state audit was requested or performed.

## LARGEST GAP

The hydrangea's individual leaf blades still read as rigid, opaque lobed plates at ordinary desktop portrait size, especially the isolated low front leaves and dark upper tips. Connected branching and irregular placement are present, but the visible leaf surfaces do not yet meet the required supple, irregular curvature and plausible backface response.
